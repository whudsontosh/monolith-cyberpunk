# Changelog

## 1.2.0

Vehicles, from "Vehicles in FNFF" (pg. 113) and the vehicles in the gear list.

- **Garage:** vehicles bought in the gear shop go to a Garage on the Gear & cash tab instead of the gear list,
  each with its skill, handling, top speed, SP, seats and a structure track. A shop option buys them with cyber
  controls at twice the price.
- **Control rolls:** REF + Driving or Piloting + 1D10 against the maneuver: Simple 15, Difficult 20, Very
  Difficult 25. The vehicle's handling (sportscar +2, truck −4, AV-4 −2 and so on) and speed (double −2, triple
  −4, four times −6) come off automatically, and low impedance cables add +1 on cyber controls.
- **Control loss:** a failed roll rolls 1D6 on the Control Loss table with its sub-rolls: skids, slides of
  1D10×10 feet, rollovers that do 5D6 to the vehicle, and for aircraft stalls and spins with the altitude lost.
- **Damage:** hits on a vehicle have no locations. Its SP comes off (halved by AP) and the rest goes to its
  SDP; at 0 it's wrecked and can't be driven. Crashes and rams do speed ÷ 20 D6 × the weight of what you hit,
  and everyone inside takes half the dice.
- **The GM:** vehicles join the combat tracker with a driver picked from the fight; they act on the driver's
  turn, take hits and crashes, and the GM makes control rolls with the driver's REF and skill. Each runner's
  garage shows on their page, and the rules reference has the vehicle tables.
- The aerodynes, Osprey, Aerogyro, limousine, truck and boat are in the tracker from the book's vehicle pages.
  The core book gives no structure for bikes, scooters, trucks and boats; those numbers are estimates, marked
  as such, and editable by the GM.
- Vehicles print on the paper sheet.
- Fixed: two chip names didn't match their skills (Operate Heavy Machinery, Pilot (Vect. Thrust)).

## 1.1.4

The book's remaining small rules.

- **Cyberlimbs and cyberoptics:** each cyberarm, cyberleg and cyberoptic is on a side (right arm, left eye and
  so on) and holds its options: 4 on an arm, 3 on a leg, 4 on an eye, with hands, feet and video options
  counting the book's spaces, and one covering or limb armor per limb. Options need a limb or eye with room;
  the shop asks which one. The Cyberware tab shows each limb with its options.
- **Cyberlimb damage:** a limb takes 20 structural damage before it's useless and 30 before it's destroyed
  (hydraulic rams 30/40; myomar and reinforced joints +5). Hits there go to the limb, not your wounds, with no
  stun save, in the damage calculator and the GM's Apply hit. Limb armor counts as SP 20 on that limb.
- **Chipware:** buy skill chips at the book's prices per level, slot as many as your INT, and practice reflex
  chips in (2 days a level). A working chip sets the skill to its level. Needs a neural processor and plugs
  or a chipware socket.
- **Switching implants off and therapy:** any implant can be switched off, which stops its effects. With
  everything off, therapy brings back 2 EMP a week. At EMP 0 the sheet and the GM's screens show
  cyberpsychosis.
- **Adrenal booster:** a Boost button: +1 REF for 1D6+2 turns, three times a day.
- **Linear frames:** their Strength sets your lift (50 × Strength kg) and melee damage bonus.
- **Facedowns and Reputation:** a Facedown window for players and in the GM's combat tracker (COOL + Rep +
  1D10; the loser backs down or fights at −3), a reputation for cowardice, and a "Has someone heard of you?"
  roll on the Rep counter.
- **Pay:** the Lifestyle panel shows your job from the book's occupation table and a Get paid button. Rolling
  starting funds also rolls whether you just lost the job. Media and Cop pay at level 8 now match the book.
- **Drugs and saves:** effects that help one skill (Stim's Endurance, enhanced perception's Awareness) are
  added to that skill's check. Saves against poison, gas, disease and sleep drugs on the Medical tab include
  drug bonuses, toxin binders and nasal filters.
- **Weapons:** lasers dial 1D6 to 5D6 a shot from a 10D6 charge; a microwaver hit offers the side-effects
  roll; knives and shuriken are thrown up to BODY × 3 meters (grenades stay at 10 × BODY).
- **Netrunning:** defences move 5 spaces a turn like runners, with a GM override, and their reach lights up.
  Files can hold money that pays the runner who copies them.
- **The GM:** the campaign shop can hide and reprice drugs and bodyshop items, the runner's wound panel shows
  Stabilized or Dying, the runner's page shows their cyberlimbs, and the rules reference has cyberlimbs,
  chipware, Reputation, facedowns, occupations, linear frames, the microwaver table and therapy.

## 1.1.3

The rest of the book's shop.

- **Weapons:** the exotics (microwaver, laser cannon, needlegun, Power Squirt, Wombat airpistol, taser,
  compound bow and crossbow), the rest of the melee weapons (Battleglove, naginata, shuriken, switchblade,
  brass knuckles, sledgehammer), and the "Old Guns Never Die" list at about half the price of a new gun.
  Bows and crossbows use Archery; shuriken are thrown with Athletics.
- **Cyberware:** the rest of the book's list, about 80 more pieces: fashionware, neural links, eye and ear
  options, implants (gills, adrenal booster, sensors and more), cyberlimb options, hands and feet, limb
  built-ins and limb weapons, linear frames and body plating, each with its surgery code.
- **Cyberweapons in the Attack window:** installing a cyberweapon (rippers, wolvers, Hammer hand, Buzz hand,
  micro-missile launcher, capacitor laser and so on) adds it to your weapons; removing the implant removes it.
  Body plating counts as armor.
- **Gear:** the book's whole gear list, grouped like the book: fashion in its five styles, tools, personal
  electronics, data systems, communications, surveillance, security, medical, furnishings, entertainment,
  ammunition and reloads, and vehicles.
- **Living costs:** the Lifestyle panel shows the book's housing, groceries and services prices.
- Fixed prices to match the book: the flamethrower (1,500eb), knives (20eb) and clubs (free).

## 1.1.2

Trauma Team: the rulebook's medical chapter.

- **Medical tab:** a new sheet tab with the runner's condition, treatment, healing, drugs and Trauma Team.
- **Stabilization:** a mortally wounded runner makes a death save every turn until someone stabilizes them:
  TECH + First Aid or Medical Tech + 1D10 against their total damage, +5 in a hospital, +3 in a Trauma Team
  ambulance or a cryotank. Once stabilized, the death save goes away until they take another wound.
- **Death and Death State:** a failed death save means dead at the end of the turn. Death State rises by 2
  a minute; revive with 1D10 over it. At Death State 10 they're gone for good.
- **Healing:** First Aid (half a point a day) and Medical Tech (1 a day) checks, with speedheal, nanotech,
  enhanced antibodies and nanosurgeons. Untreated wounds get 2 points worse a day. A Time passes control
  heals the runner over days, and each wound level's limits while healing are shown.
- **Trauma Team:** coverage (none, a monthly account or a corporate policy), calling them (1D6+1 minutes),
  their +3, and the 100eb-a-minute bill.
- **Drugs:** the book's street drugs (SynthCoke, Stim, Syncomp 15, Speedheal, Boost, Blue Glass, Smash,
  'Dorph and Black Lace) in the shop. Taking a dose rolls its duration and side effects and changes your
  stats while it runs. Addictive drugs get hourly craving checks.
- **Drug lab:** design a drug with the book's effects, Strength, side effects and duration. The app works
  out its Difficulty and price, and you make it with a Pharmaceuticals check.
- **Bodyshop:** appearance changes, +1 ATTR sculpts, exotics, body bank and vat-grown parts (with the
  book's availability roll), body bank storage, Trauma Team accounts and nanotech treatment.
- **Surgery:** every piece of cyberware has its surgery code. Tick "Include surgery" in the shop and
  installing it rolls the surgical damage and adds the fee.
- **Cyberware prices:** the cyberware list now uses the book's prices and Humanity costs. Added Nanosurgeons.
- **The GM:** each runner's page has the medical panels; Downtime heals the whole party over a number of
  days; the combat tracker can stabilize anyone with the medic's total; the party list shows who's dying;
  and the rules reference has the medical, surgery, body bank, bodysculpt and drug tables.
- The targeting scope now gives its +1 only with smartgun attacks, as the book says.

## 1.1.1

The rest of Friday Night Firefight, the rulebook's combat chapter.

- **Attack modifiers:** the attack window has the book's situation modifiers (moving, tiny or silhouetted
  targets, ambush, firing while running, two weapons, ricochets, turrets and more), aiming for up to three
  turns, laser and telescopic sights, targeting scopes and smartgoggles, and the −3 for each extra action in
  a turn. Aimed shots, sights and scopes switch off on a fast draw or full auto.
- **Fast draw:** a Fast draw button rolls initiative at +3 and turns on the −3 snapshot penalty. The GM's
  tracker has a fast draw box for every combatant.
- **Fumble tables:** a natural 1 rolls the book's fumble table for the skill's stat (combat, athletics,
  TECH, EMP and INT skills) and shows the result. Fumbling with a gun can jam it, drop it, discharge it or
  hit yourself; automatic weapons roll their reliability.
- **Cover:** the damage calculator and the GM's tracker add cover SP (walls, car doors, trees and more)
  using the proportional armor rule.
- **Melee:** fill in the defender's total, or let the app roll it, for opposed melee. Dodging (−2 to
  attacks against them) and parrying, with blade save rolls, in the GM's tracker.
- **Martial arts:** every style in the book with its key-attack bonuses and its moves (strike, kick, throw,
  hold, choke, sweep, grapple, escape, disarm). Martial arts add their level to damage.
- **Mono-edges:** monoblades and monokatanas count soft armor at a third and hard armor at two thirds,
  double damage on a natural 10, and break on a fumble.
- **Area weapons:** grenades (fragmentation, incendiary, stun, dazzle, sonic and gas), grenade launchers,
  C-6 plastic explosive, mines and claymores, Molotov cocktails, flamethrowers, the RPG-A and the Scorpion
  16 missile launcher. Missed throws scatter by the book's table, blasts hit everything in the radius, and
  gas grenades take the book's gases. Grenades and explosives stack in your gear.
- **Shotguns:** the pattern widens and its damage drops with range; autoshotguns fire several blasts.
- **Suppressive fire:** fire rounds into a zone and the app works out the save. The GM sets the save in the
  tracker and rolls each combatant who crosses it.
- The GM's tools get a grenade scatter roll, and the rules reference has attack modifiers, cover, area
  effects, scatter, fumbles, martial arts, melee and suppressive fire.
- The Militech Arms RPG-A now costs the book's 7,500eb.

## 1.1.0

Netrunning, from the Cyberpunk 2020 rulebook's Netrunner chapter.

- **Your deck:** a Netrunning tab on the sheet with your cyberdeck (the book's decks, from the stock deck
  to the Parraline 5750 and the Kirama LPD-12), how you connect (plugs, 'trodes or keyboard), options,
  and upgrades at the book's prices: +1 Speed, +1 data walls, a second memory.
- **Programs:** every program in the book, from Hammer to Hellhound, with what it does. Load programs into
  your deck's memory or keep them on chips, and carry subroutines in Demons (half the memory, the Demon's
  Strength).
- **Net rolls:** Net initiative, log off, and a Net roll window for every program class, using the book's
  formulas, with the result tagged in the roll log.
- **Writing programs:** the Programming 101 rules, with Difficulty, size, work time and street value, and the
  INT + Programming check.
- **Data fortresses (GM):** build systems on the 20 × 20 subgrid: data walls, code gates, CPUs, memories with
  files and virtual realities, remotes, LDLs and defences. INT, AI, actions, memory and construction cost
  are worked out as you build, and the rulebook's Fast Fortress tables roll one for you.
- **Live runs:** start a run and the players' runners jack in. Each player sees the subgrid from their
  runner's position, with line of sight and explored areas, and moves and runs programs from the Net
  window. The GM's app rolls both sides: walls, gates, stealth and detection, Anti-IC, anti-personnel
  damage that lands on the runner's wounds, anti-system attacks on decks, controllers, reading and copying
  files. The GM runs the defences, initiative and turns, and can act for runners who aren't connected.
- Homebrew packs can add programs, ICE and decks. The campaign shop can hide or reprice them.
  Campaign packages carry data fortresses.
- The paper sheet gets a cyberdeck and programs page, and the rules reference a Netrunning section.
- Added the Cybermodem link (neuralware) from the cyberware chart.
